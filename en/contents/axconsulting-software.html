AX Transformation Consulting That Changes How Your Organization Works

From strategy mapping to legacy modernization, agent supply, and in-house adoption

Self Check

Check These First

If you answer “no” to any of the questions below, this program is worth a closer look. Each question describes a capability your company will have after the engagement.

When you write a proposal, does AI find your existing materials and draft it for you?

If “no,” what happens today

Finding and editing past proposals and track-record materials takes days.

When a new feature ships, does AI write its documentation for you?

If “no,” what happens today

Developers spend a day on screenshots and explanatory material, and the people who receive it come back with questions.

When development is done, are your website and product brochure updated without anyone touching them?

If “no,” what happens today

As content passes among development, marketing, and web staff, details get lost and updates arrive late.

Can you see in one place which fix went to which client, and when?

If “no,” what happens today

File-level fixes made on site never make it back to headquarters, so only the person who made them can maintain that client.

Are requirements gathered in the field recorded as specs and carried through to implementation, testing, and patches at headquarters?

If “no,” what happens today

Requirements are scattered across people's memories and messengers, so the same explanations get repeated.

When a customer sends requirements, can you answer with a working demo within days?

If “no,” what happens today

Quoting and scheduling drag on, and you end up competing on price alone.

Can you win more business without adding headcount?

If “no,” what happens today

More orders bring higher fixed costs, and when orders fall, profit and loss swing right away.

If everyone is busy but your operating margin isn't moving, the cause isn't individual speed. It's the way work is handed off.

Team collaborator

Evidence for the next operating decisionAre error and rework rates in operation within tolerance? Can it stop and recover when limits are exceeded?

Value Stream

From Personal Productivity to Organizational Performance

Even if developers write code fast, when it takes days to create and hand off the material that explains a feature, the speed customers feel stays the same. The unit of diagnosis is a single customer request.

Context lost in handoffs

When a feature is added, a developer spends a day capturing screens to build explanatory material, and a marketer holds another meeting to understand it. Once a few lines land in the product brochure, the website owner gets yet another request. Every handoff between people drops context and adds waiting.

uEngine handed this stretch to agents. Developers just push code. Agents identify the new feature from the code change, explain it in the customer's language, update the relevant section of the manual, and replace the website material with the new version.

AreaBeforeAfter
How work startsA department head orders a documentThe product owner records the customer's intent, expected benefits, and acceptance criteria as a spec
Feature documentationA developer captures screens and writes the explanationAn agent identifies the feature from the code change and writes the manual with examples drawn from real work
HandoffsEach department re-summarizes from meeting notes and messengersRequest rationale, tests, and change history are linked to the approved spec and reused
On-site fixesStaff edit files directly at the client siteThe field records requirements as specs and applies patches implemented and tested at headquarters
Performance evaluationNumber of documents written and individual hours workedTime from request intake to rollout, on-time delivery, rework rate

Measuring the impact

We compare requests of the same type and difficulty before and after. Lead time is the median elapsed time from intake to rollout; the late-delivery rate is items past the promised date ÷ items due in the period; quality is items fixed again after rollout ÷ items rolled out. We also record sample sizes and reviewer hours.

Legacy and Maintenance

Legacy Modernization and Maintenance

Code that differs from client to client and files patched one by one on site create maintenance costs that are hard to see. We replace direct on-site file edits with component-level management and design, together with you, a way to manage the product around specs.

We split the existing product into service-level components, a form of microservices architecture, and separate the fixed product core that every client shares from the client-specific areas.

Problems that go away after the shift

ProblemWhy it happens in AS-ISWhy it goes away in TO-BE
Clients only one person can modifyFiles are edited directly on site, leaving no record at headquartersEvery change is recorded by version in the repository
Clients that can't be upgradedClient customizations and product code are mixed in the same filesClient-specific areas sit behind standard interfaces, so only the core is replaced
All-hands negotiation to standardizeWhether each individual request becomes a common feature is negotiated every timeClient features are implemented separately and the product core stays intact
Fixes that break other featuresOnly the edited screen is checked on siteOnly versions that pass regression and integration testing are deployed
Hard-to-trace deployment historyFile-level patches scatter the recordsWhole versions are replaced and each client's version is recorded

Legacy modernization techniques

We analyze the business rules in the existing system, organize them into specs, and implement the new product structure from them. uEngine's Robo Analyzer and Robo Architect support this process.

StageDaily workTools
1 AnalysisAnalyze legacy code and database structure to extract relationships between functions, data handling, and business rulesRobo Analyzer
2 Spec recoveryOrganize the extracted rules into requirements and acceptance criteria for the owner to confirmRobo Analyzer, Robo Architect
3 Target designDivide business domains and components, and separate client-specific features behind standard interfacesRobo Architect
4 ImplementationGenerate and review code and tests in the chosen language (Java, Rust, Go, etc.) according to the specRobo Architect, coding agents
5 Equivalence testingCompare migration results against input and output cases from the existing systemRegression testing
6 Incremental cutoverReplace module by module, run in parallel with the existing system, and widen the scopeVersioned deployment

Clients on older versions and sites where the client has modified the source directly are investigated separately during assessment to set the migration order.

People and Adoption

From Performer to Agent Boss

An Agent Boss is a business-side owner who sets the goals and quality bar for the work, assigns tasks to agents, and is accountable for the results. They draw on their job experience to define the right inputs and the criteria for judging exceptions.

PerformerDoing the workDemonstrates their usual work and explains what separates good results from rejected ones.Output that shows the capabilityInput/output samples and a quality checklist
Skill designerDelegating the workBreaks work into small tasks and defines tools, permissions, and when to ask. Reruns them with different cases.Output that shows the capabilityReusable skills and a test set
Agent BossOwning the resultsSets the order and owners of multiple skills, reviews failure logs, and decides whether to fix and revalidate or stop using them.Output that shows the capabilityException-handling criteria and an operations scorecard

Start with a core group

First pick two or three people who believe in this way of working and give them the pilot. Once visible results appear, widen the scope so other teams follow the same approach. Executives guarantee the core group time and authority.

Roles change

Developers take on writing specs and reviewing results, and maintenance staff become consultants who understand customer needs on site and turn them into specs. Training and hands-on practice support the transition, and roles for members who find it hard are decided together with executives.

Training uses one real task from your company

Participants build skills from their own work formats and validate them with colleagues' cases. When results are off, they trace the cause to input data, instructions, tool calls, or approval criteria and fix the skill.

Make cross-department responsibilities clear

Process owners decide customer outcomes and the scope of delegation. Subject-matter experts provide quality criteria and exception cases, and IT staff manage system access and execution logs. uEngine handles the initial design and training, then helps your teams take over step by step.
