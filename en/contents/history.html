The Story Behind the Records

Opening Up Technology,
Building Together Over Time

From a small engine that expressed the flow of work as software, to the cloud and AI. Running through uEngine's journey is one belief: customers should be able to understand the technology and evolve it themselves.

The decision to open-source an engine we built ourselves came back to us as the attention of developers around the world.

2001–2006 · Beginnings and Going Open

So Everyone Could Use the Engine We Built

In 2001, uEngine began R&D on a BPM engine that expresses and executes business processes as software. The starting point was to turn recurring work flows into visible models and to build an engine that could be adapted to the way each company works.

In 2003, we released uEngine BPMS on SourceForge. We opened up even the internal structure so developers could read, modify and evolve the code together. It was a deliberate choice: the companies using the product should keep people who understand it.

2006–2011 · Into the Enterprise

The Responsibility That Comes After Open Source

Founded as a sole proprietorship in 2006 and incorporated in 2007, the open engine began taking on real enterprise work. It connected work flows across very different settings: quality management in automotive, underwriting and approvals in finance, and operations in manufacturing and logistics. Through Process Codi, we also extended the reach of BPM into human collaboration.

Being open source did not automatically solve the challenges of adoption and operations. uEngine chose to share the customer's operational burden through architecture consulting, technical support and training. Building the system and equipping customers to run it themselves have always gone hand in hand.

2012–2016 · Open Cloud Engine

Bringing Individual Technologies Together into One Platform

Our focus widened from tools for designing work to environments for developing and deploying it. uEngine Cloud Edition and a web-based IDE were attempts to let developers write code in the browser and connect all the way to the runtime environment.

In 2013, the Open Cloud Engine (OCE) alliance was formed. uEngine, uEngine Cloud, CUBRID, Cloudine, Enkisoft, MHR and WebSquared pooled their technologies in workflow, databases, WAS, big data and operations. Rather than merely extending one company's product, we built an open-source platform that connected each other's expertise.

The shared cloud pool and application-sharing environment for public R&D institutions such as the Korea Institute for Advancement of Technology (KIAT) is one example of this collaboration reaching the field. The idea of connecting analysis, design, development, deployment and operations lives on today in uEngine Cloud.

OCE · The Results of Open Source Collaboration

A Challenge That Led to Open Source Program Awards

The attempt to link individual open-source projects into a single cloud platform drew industry attention and led to awards in open source support programs. It was the moment when developing and sharing together became an achievement for both companies and the community.

Press coverage of the OCE joint development at the time ↗

2017–2022 · Cloud Native

From Moving Servers to Understanding the Work

Even with a cloud environment in place, how to break up a large system remained a hard problem. uEngine focused on domain-driven design (DDD) and EventStorming so that business and developers could understand the work together and design services along those boundaries.

That experience led to MSA Easy's modeling and code generation and Kubernetes-based deployment and operations. We wanted to create a flow in which a model describing the work leads to implementation, and the implemented services run in a real environment. It carried the original BPM idea of 'connecting work and software' into the cloud-native world.

Community and Education · Building Capability Together

Leaving Behind People Who Understand, Along with the Tools

Opening our source code led to sharing knowledge and experience. Through open-source community activities, participation in the eGovFrame Open Community, the Open Cloud Engine Conference and technical lectures, we have shared what we learned in the field.

MSA School has taught everything from domain design to cloud-native implementation. As of 2024, its record of 325 courses, 9,655 participants and 17,864 cumulative hours holds the time of developers who learned a new architecture first-hand to apply it in the field.

Time with Customers · Accumulation and Change

Relationships That Prepare for the Next Change Together

Hanwha Group's early adoption of our groupware engine and our participation in its 2010 enterprise-wide system rebuild led to MSA transition consulting for Hanwha Systems in 2021, implementation training in 2022, and the Hanwha Life BPM build awarded in 2025. It is a history of solving the same customer's new challenges with different technologies.

LG's MSA development methodology and manufacturing BPM, KB Wallet's MSA implementation consulting, POSCO's logistics platform and AI transformation, and Gov24's cloud-native consulting. Industries and systems differ, but the process of understanding the work, establishing the design and helping it run in the field has continued.

BPM case studies →

2023–Present · Continuing the Journey with AI

Back to the Starting Point: Technology That Understands the Work

Even in an era when AI writes the code, explaining what to build and how it should behave still matters. Robo Architect, which evolved from MSA Easy, connects design specifications to implementation, and Robo Modernizer helps understand and transform existing systems. Robo Architect's outputs are deployed to uEngine Cloud and carried into operations.

uEngine6 BPM and Process GPT are moving toward executing accumulated business processes together with AI. Process-GPT's GS Certification Grade 1 and registration on the Public Procurement Service Digital Service Mall in 2026, along with process asset creation and agentic automation in the SKT OSS domain, are all part of that continuum.

The names of the technologies change, but our commitment to helping enterprises understand their own work and software and change it themselves stays the same. uEngine continues that work together with open source.

Since 2003, uEngine Solutions has been a technology-driven company developing and releasing as open-source projects Business Process Management (BPM), AI, MSA, enterprise social networking, cloud platforms including PaaS, and development frameworks built around domain-driven development.
